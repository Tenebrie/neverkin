While not the flashy kind that introduces paradoxes, Neverkin time travel allows you to navigate over your timeline or move your events around with high precision. To open the time travel dialog, press the **T** key while on the Timeline.

<Image src={timeTravel01} alt="Time travel dialog" />

If you have any events selected, you will see the **Move selected events** checkbox as well. If it's toggled on, then the events will move according to the time you select. Otherwise, only your view moves.

A preview updates as you type. Press **Enter** to confirm.

## Typing a date

The simplest input is a date, written the way your calendar formats them.
If your dates look like `00:00 March 01, 2026`, you can type:

- `12:00 May 04, 2030` — that exact moment

You can also type part of a date, as long as neighbouring fields stay together:

- `14:23` — that time of day, keeping the current date
- `May 04, 2030` — that date, keeping the current time of day
- `May 04` — May the 4th of the current year
- `September` - for named units like months, this is also accepted

Anything you leave out keeps its current value.

In essence, if you type your calendar's full date format or a long enough sub-string, it should be recognized. For more ambiguous cases like the year alone, see advanced syntax below.

## Selectors

When you only want to change one thing, use a **selector** instead of writing out a full date.
The position of the number decides what happens:

- **Unit first** — `y2000` — *sets* the unit to that value (absolute)
- **Number first** — `2000y` — *steps* by that many units (relative)

<Aside type="tip">
The intuition is the way you pronounce it. To set the date to the **Year 2000**, it's `y2000`. If you want to move by **2000 years**, it's `2000y`.
</Aside>

### Setting a value

| Selector | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `m15` | move to the 15th minute of the current hour |
| `h6` | move to the 6th hour of the current day |
| `d3` | move to the 3rd day of the current month |
| `y2000` | move to year 2000 |
| `y-2000` | move to year -2000 |
| `M3` | move to April (the fourth month) |

Months are zero-indexed: `M0` is January, `M3` is April.

### Stepping by an amount

| Selector | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `15m` | 15 minutes forward |
| `6h` | 6 hours forward |
| `3d` | 3 days forward |
| `3M` | 3 months forward |
| `1y` | 1 year forward |
| `-10y` | 10 years back |

Negative numbers step backwards. Setting a value has no direction — `y2000` lands on the year 2000 regardless of where you start.

## Combining selectors

Separate selectors with spaces. They apply left to right:

- `y2000 M3 d1` — 1st of April, 2000
- `3d 15m` — three days and fifteen minutes from now
- `y2000 -1d` — the same date in the year 2000, minus one day

Absolute and relative selectors mix freely.

## Exact selectors

Adding `!` after a selector matches the start of that unit:

- `y2000` — year 2000, keeping the current month, day and time
- `y2000!` — 00:00 on the 1st of January, 2000

`d1!` gives you midnight on the 1st; `h6!` gives you 6:00 sharp.

## Unit names

The single letters above are shorthands. Each unit also accepts its full name and short name, so `y2000`, `yr2000` and `year2000` all do the same thing.

For single-letter shorthands, case matters when two units share a letter. In the Gregorian calendar, minutes and months are both `m` — minute is lowercase, month is uppercase. Day (`d` or `D`) works either way.

<Aside type="note" title="Selectors come from your calendar">
Shorthands and names come from the world's calendar, not a built-in list. A calendar with a `sol` unit accepts `3sol`, while one without weeks won't understand `1w`.

If a selector doesn't match any unit in your calendar, it's silently ignored — the preview won't move. Check the previewed time if something seems off.
</Aside>

## Built-in calendars

As the selectors are slightly different for each calendar definition, you may refer to the following tables for reference about the built-in calendars.

### Gregorian Calendar (Earth)

Date format: `hh:mm MM DD, YYYY`

| Unit | Shorthand | Also accepts | Values |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minute | `m` | `min`, `minute` | 0-59 |
| Hour | `h` | `hr`, `hour` | 0-23 |
| Day | `d` | `day` | 1-31 |
| Month | `M` | `mon`, `month` | 0-11, or a month name |
| Year | `y` | `yr`, `year` | any non-zero year |

**Months** — `January`, `February`, `March`, `April`, `May`, `June`, `July`, `August`, `September`, `October`, `November`, `December`.

### Darian Calendar (Martian)

| Unit | Shorthand | Also accepts | Values |
|---|---|---|---|

| Hour | `h` | `hr`, `hour` | 0-23 |
| Sol | `s` | `sol` | 1-28 |
| Month | `M` | `mon`, `month` | 0-23, or a month name |
| Year | `y` | `yr`, `year` | any year |
  
**Months** — `Sagittarius`, `Dhanus`, `Capricornus`, `Makara`, `Aquarius`, `Kumbha`, `Pisces`, `Mina`, `Aries`, `Mesha`, `Taurus`, `Rishabha`, `Gemini`, `Mithuna`, `Cancer`, `Karka`, `Leo`, `Simha`, `Virgo`, `Kanya`, `Libra`, `Tula`, `Scorpius`, `Vrishika`.
